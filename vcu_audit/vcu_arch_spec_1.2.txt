            1.1          Introduction
            The Everest MMD (Multi-Media Distributed) Video CODEC Unit (‘VCU’) provides multi-standard video encoding and
            decoding, including h.265 (HEVC) and h.264 (AVC) standards. The unit contains an encode (compress) unit and a
            separate decode (decompress) unit and is capable of concurrent encoding and decoding at rates up to 4Kx2Kp60.

            The VCU is a hardened block, containing 1 instance of a multi-core encoder IP and 1 instance of multi-core decoder
            IP, and related logic. It shall be designed to fit within the footprint of two GTY quad modules and can replace GTY
            quad pairs along the right edge of an Everest (Telluride) SoC device. For general details regarding MMD technology
            refer to the Everest MMD general architecture specifications document.

            The MMD VCU is part of the Telluride family of products. The MMD VCU is sometimes referred to as VCU2, to
            distinguish it from the original Diablo (Zynq Ultrascale+) VCU.

            The VCU has a direct connection to the NoC along its right edge and to the PL along its left edge.

            Target process technology is 7nm.

            Changes relative to Diablo VCU include

                     -    Improved encoding quality
                     -    Re-architected cores for higher efficiency and lower bandwidth
                     -    2 encoder cores per encoder instance
                     -    Addition of frame buffer compression
                     -    Hardened encoder ‘L2 cache’
                     -    Interlaced support
                     -    4:4:4 color format support
                     -    12 bit per color support

            1.5.2 8K Support
            The VCU is primarily targeting 4Kx2K as its maximum video resolution. Some 8K (7680x4320) support is
            available, with limitations and quality tradeoffs.

            1.5.2.1 8K using Single VCU Instance
            One VCU encoder instance shall support 8Kp15 encoding using both cores, each core encoding a 4Kx4K
            tile.

            One VCU decoder instance is not required to support 8Kp15 decoding.

            1.5.2.2 8K using 2 VCU Instances
            In SoCs with 2 VCU instances, 8Kp30 encode/decode shall be supported using ‘multi-instance’ mode in
            which 2 VCU instances jointly encode/decode a single 8K stream (and produce a valid 8K bitstream) by
            each encoding/decoding a 4Kx4K tile. An additional low speed and low signal count interface may be
            required between the 2 VCU instances and shall be routed via PL resources.

            In multi-instance mode, each VCU instance processes a 4Kx4K tile, and there is no data sharing or
            deblocking possible along the vertical boundary between the 2 tiles. This may lead to some quality
            degradation, or visible artifacts along this boundary if encoding bit rate is not sufficiently high.

            1.5.3 Solution to MCU Address Space Limitation
            The VCU MCUs have a limited 40-bit address space while the SoC has a 48-bit address space. To solve
            the problem, 3 approaches are considered.

            a) Expand address space by appending a 48-bit base address
            b) Use NMU remap function to map multiple independent apertures
            c) Use a custom designed MMU
            Option (a) is simple but limits all MCU access to a single 1TB aperture. Option (b) relies on existing NMU
            functionality and allows up to 32 independent apertures to be remapped. Option (c) is similar to (b) but
            decouples the MMU from the NMU.

            Option (b) is preferred. For easy implementation, the 2 MCU AXI master ports should be routed to a
            dedicated NoC NMU, separate from the NMUs used by the encode/decode cores.

            1.5.4 Physical Shape, Modularity, Reusability
            VCU is a 2x tall MMD tile (replacing 2 GT quads). See MMD general arch specs for details.

            1.5.5.1 DCI Support
            The DCI (Digital Cinema) 4K format is 4096x2160, whereas standard UHD 4K video format is 3840x2160.
            The DCI 4K pixel rate is therefore approx. 6.7% faster than UHD 4K. The VCU shall support DCI 4K format.
            Therefore, target frequencies are increased by 6.7%, as shown in the table below.

                Module                            UHD 4K required clock rate        DCI 4K required clock rate
                                                  [MHz]                             [MHz]
                Encoder                           890                               950
                Decoder                           860                               918


            1.5.5.2 Stretch Goals
            The following stretch goals should be evaluated for feasibility.

                  -   Encoder stretch goal of 1120MHz can provide 4Kp120 encode at 4:2:0
                         o This target is 18% higher than POR (950MHz)
                  -   Decoder stretch goal of 940MHz can provide 4Kp120 decode at 4:2:0
                         o This target is 2.3% higher than POR (918MHz)

            1.5.5.3 Real-Time vs Non-Real-Time Operation
            Real-time operation is characterized by a fixed encode/decode latency and the need to complete each
            frame encode/decode within precisely one frame period (e.g. 16msec). However, there may be use
            cases in which non-real-time operation is acceptable, meaning that both the processing time and latency
            can be variable. In such cases, the encoder/decoder may ‘slow down’ when a worst-case frame is
            encountered, and ‘speed up’ during typical frames.

            1.5.6 Encoder-Decoder Separation and Isolation
            Encoder and decoder are often thought of as operating together. However, there are cases in which
            encoder and decoder operation is completely independent and associated with different and
            independent applications. One example may be a decoder used for stored video playback while the
            encoder is used for live video capture. It is therefore necessary to maintain encoder-decoder separation
            and isolation.

            Although sharing a physical tile, the encoder and decoder functions are completely independent and the
            tile design shall maximize separation and isolation of these two functions, such that normal operation of
            each function will be completely un-affected and un-interrupted by any operations and events within
            the other function, including:

                  -   Hard reset
                  -   Soft reset
                  -   Clock rate changes
                  -   Clock gating
                  -   Full or partial re-initialization
                  -   MCU reset and initialization
                  -   Error recovery procedures including NMU reset and re-initialization

            1.6   VCU Structure
            The VCU is a hardened block designed by Xilinx. It shall contain the encoder and decoder vendor IP. In
            addition, the VCU shall contain additional modules or functions needed to meet the above requirements
            and facilitate the interface between the vendor IP and the NoC/PL.

                • AXI interconnect, register slices, async bridge(s)
                • NMU, NSU (if not provided by the NoC)
                • NPI
                • Clock/power manager
                • Performance monitors
                • Debug and test
            Encoder and decoder operation shall be completely independent, e.g., each may operate or be idle or
            disabled or reset independently without affecting the other, and each shall have independent clocks.

            1.9       Operation

            1.9.1 Encoder
            Typical operation involves live video captured in frame buffers in DRAM. The encoder reads the current
            frame along with 0, 1, or 2 additional reference frames from frame buffers for encoding. The encoded
            bit-stream is written back to memory and is available for transmission, storage, or further processing
            (e.g. interleaving with compressed audio and creating a transport stream). The encoder operates at the
            slice level only. A small amount of additional work is required by the CPU to create a fully compliant
            transport stream or program stream. The encoder includes a cache for reducing DRAM bandwidth by
            reusing data fetched for motion estimation and other purposes.

            1.9.2 Decoder
            Operation is memory-to-memory. The decoder reads the compressed bit-stream from memory, and,
            along with 0, 1, or 2 additional reference frames from frame buffers, decodes the current frame and



            11
[Confidential - AMD Internal Distribution Only]

                                              Everest MMD VCU Architecture Specs


            stores it in a frame buffer. The decoder operates at the slice level only. A small amount of additional
            work is required by the CPU to de-multiplex the transport stream and create slice-level tasks for the
            decoder. The decoded video is read for display or further processing by other entities (not part of VDEC).

            1.10 Use Models
            Typical VCU use cases include:

                 •   Single stream encode/decode
                 •   Multi-stream encode/decode at lower resolutions
                 •   Multi- stream encode/decode at different encoding standards, e.g. h.264 and h.265

            1.11 Memory Bandwidth Requirements

            1.11.1 General
            In general, max memory bandwidth requirement is increased relative to Diablo VCU due to the
            increased amount of processing for 4:4:4 format (50% higher than 4:2:2), and increased color
            component word size from 10 bits to 12 bits (20% increase). Frame buffer compression can help reduce
            average bandwidth but does not help in case of worst-case frames in which compression is not effective.

            1.11.2 Encoder
            Expected max encoder bandwidth at 4Kp60 4:4:4 12bit is ~6GB/s with L2 cache enabled.

            This is bandwidth required by the encoder itself. At a minimum, additional bandwidth must be reserved
            for the raw video being written to DRAM before compression, which is about ~1GB/s per stream at
            4Kp60 4:2:0 10-bit, or 2.2GB/s per stream at 4Kp60 4:4:4 12 bits.




            Note: FBC is enabled on all but the input frame.


            12
[Confidential - AMD Internal Distribution Only]

                                              Everest MMD VCU Architecture Specs


            1.11.3 Decoder
            Expected max decoder bandwidth at 4Kp60 4:4:4 12bit is ~10-15GB/s.

            This is bandwidth required by the decoder itself. At a minimum, additional bandwidth must be reserved
            for the decompressed video being read out for display or further processing, which is about ~1GB/s per
            decoded stream at 4Kp60 4:2:0 10-bit, or 2.2GB/s per stream at 4Kp60 4:4:4 12 bits.




            Note: FBC is disabled.

            1.11.4 Encoder L2 Cache Limitations
            The encoder L2 cache is sized at 1152KB, which is optimal for 4Kp60 encoding at 12 bit and 4:4:4. The
            encoder max bandwidth is reduced by up to 33% thanks to the cache. However, in some usage scenarios
            the L2 cache is less effective, as explained below.

            1.11.4.1 Ultra-Low Latency mode
            In this mode, each of the 2 encoder cores within one encoder instance is used to encode a separate
            4Kp30 stream, and both cores share the L2 cache. However, the L2 cache is sized for the normal case of
            both encoder cores sharing a single 4Kx2K stream, each encoding one half. Therefore, the L2 cache is
            under-sized for this case, and 2 mitigation choices are available

                 -   Reduce motion estimation search range to match the cache size limitation (possibly resulting in
                     lower encoding quality)
                 -   Keep the search range, but accept a bandwidth increase due to the lower cache hit rate

            1.11.4.2 8K Encoding
            Similar to the case above, when encoding 8Kx4K streams, the cache size is inadequate, and the same 2
            mitigation choices are available

            1.11.4.3 Reduced Limitations
            The cache limitations discussed above apply to the max use case, and these limitations may be reduced
            to a varying extent in the following cases

                 -   Resolutions less than 8K/4K
                 -   4:2:2 or 4:2:0 formats (rather than 4:4:4)
                 -   8 bit or 10 bit rather than 12 bit (TBD)

            1.18 VCU Power Domain
            The VCU shall have its own dedicated VCCINT power domain, and associated power supply die micro-
            bumps and possibly package pins. The VCU power rail may be merges with one or more device rails at
            the package or board level.

            This key decision is driven by the following requirements:

                 -  Ability to run the VCU at a voltage different from the PL voltage for power/performance point
                    reasons
                 - Ability to power down the VCU externally when not needed
            There will be no power gating mechanism within the VCU block. Power down can be achieved via an
            external power regulator (if dedicated power pins exist), or by permanently tying VCU power to ground
            in the package (e.g. derived die with ‘dark’ silicon).

            1.18.1 VCU Power Rail Sharing
            The plan of record for Telluride is to share VCU power rail with TBD at the package or the board level.
            However, future devices containing VCU may have other sharing plans. Therefore, the VCU design shall
            support an independent power domain, as well as isolation/clamp/levelshifting.

            1.19 Memory BIST
            Same as Diablo VCU.

            1.20 VCU Disable
            Permanent disable via 2 x E-FUSE, one for encoder and one for decoder. The cached E-FUSE values shall
            be provided via NPI as part of the boot process. If more than one VCU instance exists, each instance
            shall be independently programmable to be enabled or disabled. After reset, by default, VCU shall be
            disabled until E-FUSE value is transmitted via NPI serial side channel.

            1.21 Power Supplies
            The VCU requires only VCCINT_VCU, core power. There are no analog resources, PLLs, or IOs in the VCU.
            Note that for isolation, clamp, level shifting and POR, there will be 3 additional small power domains:

                 -   VCCINT for interface to the PL and NPI
                 -   VCCINT_BRAM for clock signals arriving from the DPLL in the GTCC
                 -   VCCINT_SOC for interface to the NoC

            1.22 Power-On Reset
            A POR circuit is required, similar to Diablo VCU, to bring the VCU to a known, safe state before PL
            configuration, regardless of rail power sequencing.

            The VCU requires isolation, clamp and level shifters (ICLS). ICLS is required because the VCU has its own

            1.30.4 Global Signals and Chip Pervasive
            Must comply with Everest global signals and chip-pervasive methodology.

            1.30.5 NPI
            The VCU shall take advantage and reuse NPI end points from the GT clocking columns that were
            originally intended for GTY configuration. NPI programmable registers shall provide basic initialization
            functionality. During normal operation, encoder programming and software driver interaction shall use
            the NoC NSU path, not the NPI and shall use PL interrupts to the APU.

            In addition to register access, the VCU can use NPI interrupts 8, 9, and 10, which are normally used by
            the GT that the VCU replaces. Interrupts 8 and 9 are for general messages and correctable error
            indication, and Interrupt 10 is used for non-correctable or fatal error notification. The VCU shall use the
            NPI interrupts as part of the initialization procedure. The VCU can optionally also use these interrupts
            for normal operation or use NoC-transported interrupts. However, the plan of record is to use PL-based
            interrupts as done by the Diablo VCU.

            1.30.6 Clocking
            The VCU has no internal PLLs. Clocks shall be provided by DPLLs in the GTCC.

            1.35 Frame Buffer Formats
            The VCU supports 3 frame buffer formats:

                 - Uncompressed raster
                 - Uncompressed tiled (64x4 or 32x4)
                 - Compressed tile
            Compressed tile format is the most bandwidth-efficient format and is internally used by the encoder for
            its reference frames.

            For the encoder:

                 -  Default input buffer format: uncompressed tile
                 -  Optional input buffer format: uncompressed raster
                        o Requires more memory bandwidth than tile if 10 or 12 bit color, due to inefficient
                            packing of each 10/12-bit value within a 16-bit word
            For the decoder:

                 -   Default output buffer format: uncompressed tile
                 -   Optional output buffer format: uncompressed raster
                        o Requires a 2nd copy of the output frame to be written in raster format, at the cost of
                             extra memory bandwidth


            The uncompressed tile format can be used to share video between the VCU and the rest of the system.
            Encoder input frames and decoder output frames shall be in this format. Any other system agent that
            produces/consumes these buffers must support the VCU tile format.

            The uncompressed raster format is the least efficient in memory bandwidth. 10 and 12-bit pixel color
            components are contained in 16-bit words, leading to storage and bandwidth waste/overhead. This
            mode can be used by the VCU, but will increase the VCU memory bandwidth requirements. On the
            other hand, raster mode may be more easily shared by other video agents in the system.

            Details of the tiled format and other buffer formats are available in a separate document.

            Note that 4:2:0 and 4:2:2 formats use semi-planar buffers (one buffer for Y, one buffer for interleaved
            Cb/Cr), while 4:4:4 format uses planar buffers (3 separate buffers for Y, Cb, Cr).

            3.3       Example of Clock Generation using DPLL
            The VCU core clock and MCU clock is generated by the DPLL, as described above. Below is an example
            how clocks can be generated.

                  -    Desired clock pair: 667 and 444 MHz (“-1mp”)
                  -    The DPLL ref clock is 133.333MHz. It is the ‘DDR’ ref clock generated by the PMC, then routed
                       via the HNOC as ‘hsr_ref_clock’, then routed up the GTCC as ‘cpm_ref_clock’ into the DPLL ref
                       clock input
                  -    The DPLL specs are:
                            o Input divider range 1-123
                            o Feedback divider range 10-400
                            o 4 output dividers range 1-123
                            o VCO freq range 2-4GHz
                            o Divided ref clock min freq 10MHz


                               Desired Freq    Inp. Div      Feedback Div     Output Div     VCO Freq       Output Freq
             Core Clock        667             1             30               6              4000           666.667
             MCU clock         444             1             30               9              4000           444.444


            Note that the DPLL’s 4 outputs share the same PLL input divider, VCO, and feedback divider, so the MCU
            clock solution can differ from the core clock solutions only by the output divider value.

            Another example for 800/555 pair. This example shows that 555 cannot be achieved, the nearest is 571.

                               Desired Freq    Inp. Div      Feedback Div     Output Div     VCO Freq       Output Freq
             Core Clock        800             1             30               5              4000           800
             MCU clock         555             1             30               7              4000           571


            Another example for 500/333 pair.

                               Desired Freq    Inp. Div      Feedback Div     Output Div     VCO Freq       Output Freq
             Core Clock        800             1             30               8              4000           500
             MCU clock         555             1             30               12             4000           333.333
